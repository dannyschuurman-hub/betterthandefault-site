An Evening Phone Routine You Can Try

Dit artikel is alleen in het Engels beschikbaar. Hieronder staat een korte samenvatting in het Nederlands. Dat is geen vertaling van het hele artikel. Geen medisch advies.

Samenvatting

In een proef met 38 studenten viel de groep die de laatste 30 minuten voor het slapengaan geen telefoon gebruikte sneller in slaap. De publicatie noemt twee verschillende looptijden voor de proef. Enquêtes naar telefoongebruik en een labproef met een e-reader geven aanvullende aanwijzingen over schermgebruik rond bedtijd. De avondroutine in dit artikel is een voorstel, geen belofte of medisch advies. ‘Phone away 30 minutes before bed’ krijgt het label ‘Small study’.

Bronnen

De bronnen staan in het Engels, zoals in het artikel. We vermelden alleen studies die we zelf hebben geopend en gelezen.

  1. He, J.-W., Tu, Z.-H., Xiao, L., Su, T., & Tang, Y.-X. (2020). Effect of restricting bedtime mobile phone use on sleep, arousal, mood, and working memory: A randomized pilot trial. PLoS ONE, 15(2), e0228756. DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0228756. PubMed: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32040492/. Read: full text (journals.plos.org, summarised on opening) and abstract (via Europe PMC). What it shows: in 38 randomised participants, a 30-minute pre-bed restriction went with better sleep measures, arousal, mood and working memory. What it does not show: results in a larger or less motivated group; the duration is inconsistent within the paper (four weeks in the abstract, one week in Methods).
  2. Exelmans, L., & Van den Bulck, J. (2016). Bedtime mobile phone use and sleep in adults. Social Science & Medicine, 148, 93-101. DOI: 10.1016/j.socscimed.2015.11.037. PubMed: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26688552/. Read: abstract (via Europe PMC). What it shows: in 844 adults, bedtime phone use went with worse sleep scores. What it does not show: cause and effect.
  3. Chang, A.-M., Aeschbach, D., Duffy, J. F., & Czeisler, C. A. (2015). Evening use of light-emitting eReaders negatively affects sleep, circadian timing, and next-morning alertness. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 112(4), 1232-1237. DOI: 10.1073/pnas.1418490112. PubMed: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25535358/. Read: abstract (via Europe PMC). What it shows: reading on a light-emitting e-book device delayed sleep onset and the circadian clock compared with a printed book. What it does not show: sample size (not in the abstract), or effects of a phone.
  4. Carter, B., Rees, P., Hale, L., Bhattacharjee, D., & Paradkar, M. S. (2016). Association between portable screen-based media device access or use and sleep outcomes: A systematic review and meta-analysis. JAMA Pediatrics, 170(12), 1202-1208. DOI: 10.1001/jamapediatrics.2016.2341. PubMed: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27802500/. Read: abstract (via Europe PMC). What it shows: in 20 cross-sectional studies (125,198 children and teenagers, mean age 14.5), bedtime device use went with inadequate sleep quantity (odds ratio 2.17). What it does not show: results for adults, or cause and effect.
  5. Olson, J. A., Sandra, D. A., Chmoulevitch, D., Raz, A., & Veissiere, S. P. L. (2022). A nudge-based intervention to reduce problematic smartphone use: Randomised controlled trial. International Journal of Mental Health and Addiction, 1-23. DOI: 10.1007/s11469-022-00826-w. PubMed: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35600564/. Read: abstract (via Europe PMC). What it shows: a ten-strategy package improved sleep quality versus a monitoring control (N = 70). What it does not show: which strategy mattered.
