Deep Work for Beginners: Start Smaller Than You Think

Dit artikel is alleen in het Engels beschikbaar. Hieronder staat een korte samenvatting in het Nederlands. Dat is geen vertaling van het hele artikel. Geen medisch advies.

Samenvatting

Lang aan één taak werken vraagt concentratie. In experimenten kostte het tijd om van taak te wisselen. Een kort plan om een onderbroken taak later weer op te pakken hielp deelnemers om hun aandacht bij de tussentaak te houden. Uit de studies in dit artikel volgt geen ideale duur voor een werkblok. Probeer bijvoorbeeld 30 minuten. Dat advies krijgt het label ‘Our advice, not proven’.

Bronnen

De bronnen staan in het Engels, zoals in het artikel. We vermelden alleen studies die we zelf hebben geopend en gelezen.

  1. Rubinstein, J. S., Meyer, D. E., & Evans, J. E. (2001). Executive control of cognitive processes in task switching. Journal of Experimental Psychology: Human Perception and Performance, 27(4), 763-797. DOI: 10.1037/0096-1523.27.4.763. PubMed: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11518143/. Read: abstract (via Europe PMC). What it shows: in four lab experiments, switching tasks took extra time, more with complex rules and less with cues. What it does not show: sample sizes (not in the abstract), or effects in office work.
  2. Leroy, S. (2009). Why is it so hard to do my work? The challenge of attention residue when switching between work tasks. Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes, 109(2), 168-181. DOI: 10.1016/j.obhdp.2009.04.002. Read: abstract (RePEc page), summarised on opening. Not listed in PubMed. What it shows: two experiments in which an unfinished task left attention behind. What it does not show: sample size (not in the abstract).
  3. Leroy, S., & Glomb, T. M. (2018). Tasks interrupted: How anticipating time pressure on resumption of an interrupted task causes attention residue and low performance on interrupting tasks and how a "ready-to-resume" plan mitigates the effects. Organization Science, 29, 380-397. DOI: 10.1287/orsc.2017.1184. Read: abstract (Semantic Scholar record). Not listed in PubMed. What it shows: across four studies, a ready-to-resume plan reduced attention residue. What it does not show: sample sizes (not in the abstract).
  4. Ophir, E., Nass, C., & Wagner, A. D. (2009). Cognitive control in media multitaskers. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 106(37), 15583-15587. DOI: 10.1073/pnas.0903620106. PubMed: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19706386/. Read: abstract (via Europe PMC). What it shows: heavy media multitaskers were more susceptible to interference and worse at a task-switching test. What it does not show: cause and effect, or sample sizes (not in the abstract).
  5. Uncapher, M. R., & Wagner, A. D. (2018). Minds and brains of media multitaskers: Current findings and future directions. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 115(40), 9889-9896. DOI: 10.1073/pnas.1611612115. PubMed: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30275312/. Read: abstract (via Europe PMC). What it shows: a review in which heavier media multitaskers often, but not always, performed worse. What it does not show: whether media multitasking causes the differences.
  6. Biwer, F., Wiradhany, W., Oude Egbrink, M. G. A., & de Bruin, A. B. H. (2023). British Journal of Educational Psychology, 93(Suppl 2), 353-367. DOI: 10.1111/bjep.12593. PubMed: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36859717/. Read: abstract (via Europe PMC). What it shows: in one day with 87 students, planned breaks went with better mood and similar task completion in less time than self-regulated breaks. What it does not show: effects beyond one day or for other groups. (Title left out because it contains a technique's brand name.)
  7. Albulescu, P., Macsinga, I., Rusu, A., Sulea, C., Bodnaru, A., & Tulbure, B. T. (2022). "Give me a break!" A systematic review and meta-analysis on the efficacy of micro-breaks for increasing well-being and performance. PLoS ONE, 17(8), e0272460. DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0272460. PubMed: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36044424/. Read: abstract (via Europe PMC). What it shows: 22 samples, N = 2,335; small effects on vigor and fatigue, non-significant effect on performance. What it does not show: a best break length.
