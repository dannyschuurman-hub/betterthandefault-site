Doomscrolling and the Feed: What Helps and What We Cannot Say

Dit artikel is in het Engels. Hieronder staat een korte Nederlandse samenvatting. Het is geen vertaling van het hele artikel en geen medisch advies.

Samenvatting

Een oneindige feed heeft geen natuurlijk einde, en kleine drempels (een pauze, een extra tik, een dagelijkse limiet) hebben in studies enig bewijs achter zich. Een dagelijkse limiet op social apps heeft een gerandomiseerde proef met studenten achter zich, maar het icoon van het beginscherm halen is ons eigen advies. Over dopamine doen we geen uitspraken, en dit is geen medisch advies.

Bronnen

De bronnen staan in het Engels, zoals in het artikel. We vermelden alleen studies die we zelf hebben geopend en gelezen.

  1. Satici, S. A., Gocet Tekin, E., Deniz, M. E., & Satici, B. (2023). Doomscrolling scale: Its association with personality traits, psychological distress, social media use, and wellbeing. Applied Research in Quality of Life. DOI: 10.1007/s11482-022-10110-7. PubMed: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36275044/. Read: abstract (via Europe PMC). What it shows: a doomscrolling questionnaire built across three studies (N = 378, 419, 460), linked to distress, fear of missing out and well-being measures. What it does not show: causation; all three studies are questionnaire snapshots.
  2. Grüning, D. J., Riedel, F., & Lorenz-Spreen, P. (2023). Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences. DOI: 10.1073/pnas.2213114120. PubMed: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36795756/. Read: abstract (via Europe PMC). What it shows: in a six-week field experiment (N = 280), app openings fell 57 percent after six weeks; in an online experiment (N = 500), the option to dismiss mattered most. What it does not show: effects against a control group in the field part (none described in the abstract), or effects beyond six weeks. (The app's name appears only in the paper's own title.)
  3. Hunt, M. G., Marx, R., Lipson, C., & Young, J. (2018). No more FOMO: Limiting social media decreases loneliness and depression. Journal of Social and Clinical Psychology. DOI: 10.1521/jscp.2018.37.10.751. Read: abstract (via the Semantic Scholar record; the publisher page refused automated access). Not listed in PubMed. What it shows: in a randomised trial of 143 undergraduates over three weeks, limiting three platforms to 10 minutes each per day lowered loneliness and depression scores versus usual use. What it does not show: results for non-students, longer periods, or other apps.
  4. Brown, L., & Kuss, D. J. (2020). Fear of missing out, mental wellbeing, and social connectedness: A seven-day social media abstinence trial. International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health, 17(12), 4566. DOI: 10.3390/ijerph17124566. PubMed: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32599962/. Read: abstract (via Europe PMC). What it shows: with 61 people before and after seven days without social media, well-being and connectedness rose and phone use fell. What it does not show: whether a control group would have looked the same; the design had none.
