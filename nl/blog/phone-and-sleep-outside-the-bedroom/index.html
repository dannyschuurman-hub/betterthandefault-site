Phone and Sleep: Charging Outside the Bedroom and Putting It Away Before Bed

Dit artikel is in het Engels. Hieronder staat een korte Nederlandse samenvatting. Het is geen vertaling van het hele artikel en geen medisch advies.

Samenvatting

Eén kleine gerandomiseerde proef (38 mensen) vond dat 30 minuten voor bed de telefoon weglaten samenging met betere slaapmaten, maar de proef was klein en de duur wordt in het artikel niet consistent genoemd. Enquêtes bij volwassenen en jongeren laten zien dat telefoongebruik bij bedtijd en slechtere slaap samengaan, maar die kunnen oorzaak en gevolg niet scheiden. Daarom krijgt "30 minuten weg" het label "Small study" en "opladen buiten de slaapkamer" het label "Our advice, not proven".

Bronnen

De bronnen staan in het Engels, zoals in het artikel. We vermelden alleen studies die we zelf hebben geopend en gelezen.

  1. He, J.-W., Tu, Z.-H., Xiao, L., Su, T., & Tang, Y.-X. (2020). Effect of restricting bedtime mobile phone use on sleep, arousal, mood, and working memory: A randomized pilot trial. PLoS ONE, 15(2), e0228756. DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0228756. PubMed: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32040492/. Read: full text (journals.plos.org, summarised on opening) and abstract (via Europe PMC). What it shows: in 38 randomised participants, a 30-minute pre-bed restriction went with better sleep measures, arousal, mood and working memory. What it does not show: results in a larger or less motivated group; the sleep measures were diary and questionnaire, and the duration is inconsistent within the paper (four weeks in the abstract, one week in Methods).
  2. Exelmans, L., & Van den Bulck, J. (2016). Bedtime mobile phone use and sleep in adults. Social Science & Medicine. DOI: 10.1016/j.socscimed.2015.11.037. PubMed: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26688552/. Read: abstract (via Europe PMC). What it shows: in 844 Flemish adults, bedtime phone use predicted worse self-reported sleep outcomes, with differences by age. What it does not show: cause and effect, because it is a cross-sectional survey from a time of lower smartphone ownership.
  3. Carter, B., Rees, P., Hale, L., Bhattacharjee, D., & Paradkar, M. S. (2016). Association between portable screen-based media device access or use and sleep outcomes: A systematic review and meta-analysis. JAMA Pediatrics. DOI: 10.1001/jamapediatrics.2016.2341. PubMed: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27802500/. Read: abstract (via Europe PMC). What it shows: across 20 cross-sectional studies (125,198 children aged 6 to 19), device use or access at bedtime was associated with poorer sleep outcomes. What it does not show: that removing devices improves sleep, or anything about adults; heterogeneity between studies was high.
