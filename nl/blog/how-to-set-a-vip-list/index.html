How to Set a VIP List: Let the Right People Through, Silence the Rest

Dit artikel is alleen in het Engels beschikbaar. Hieronder staat een korte samenvatting in het Nederlands. Dat is geen vertaling van het hele artikel. Geen medisch advies.

Samenvatting

In Focus kun je meldingen en oproepen van een paar mensen toestaan. Welke andere meldingen doorkomen, hangt af van je instellingen. Onderzoekers zagen dat meldingen een aandachtstaak konden verstoren. In een kleine proef voelden sommige deelnemers zich zonder meldingen minder bereikbaar. De aangehaalde studies testten geen VIP-lijst. Daarom krijgt deze tip het label ‘Our advice, not proven’. De app laat zien waar je de instellingen vindt.

Bronnen

De bronnen staan in het Engels, zoals in het artikel. We vermelden alleen studies die we zelf hebben geopend en gelezen.

  1. Pielot, M., & Rello, L. (2017). Productive, anxious, lonely: 24 hours without push notifications. arXiv preprint, version 3 (first posted 2016). DOI: 10.48550/arXiv.1612.02314 (the conference version has DOI 10.1145/3098279.3098526; not opened). Read: abstract page on arXiv, summarised on opening. What it shows: with notifications off for 24 hours, 30 volunteers felt less distracted and more productive, some felt anxious about slow replies and less connected, and about half were still following through two years later. What it does not show: effects beyond a day, or anything about sender lists.
  2. Fitz, N., Kushlev, K., Jagannathan, R., Lewis, T., Paliwal, D., & Ariely, D. (2019). Batching smartphone notifications can improve well-being. Computers in Human Behavior, 101, 84-94. DOI: 10.1016/j.chb.2019.07.016. Read: full text (open PDF copy). Not listed in PubMed. What it shows: batching three times a day went with feeling more attentive, in a better mood and more in control; no notifications went with more anxiety. What it does not show: sorting by sender, or effects beyond two weeks.
  3. Stothart, C., Mitchum, A., & Yehnert, C. (2015). The attentional cost of receiving a cell phone notification. Journal of Experimental Psychology: Human Perception and Performance, 41(4), 893-897. DOI: 10.1037/xhp0000100. PubMed: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26121498/. Read: abstract (via Europe PMC). What it shows: notifications alone disrupted performance on an attention-demanding task. What it does not show: sample size (not in the abstract), or real-life effects.
  4. Olson, J. A., Sandra, D. A., Chmoulevitch, D., Raz, A., & Veissiere, S. P. L. (2022). A nudge-based intervention to reduce problematic smartphone use: Randomised controlled trial. International Journal of Mental Health and Addiction, 1-23. DOI: 10.1007/s11469-022-00826-w. PubMed: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35600564/. Read: abstract (via Europe PMC). What it shows: a ten-strategy package, including disabling non-essential notifications, lowered problematic use and screen time versus a monitoring control (N = 70). What it does not show: which strategy mattered.
